### Intellectual Immodesty
Will and Avaneesh[^1] are sick of hearing me talk about is how much I despise Unified and what it did to my mental health. I spent sooooo much time dreading structures and thermo last year and how much of it was earned is up for debate[^2]. A large part of it was certainly dealing with people who'd complain and complain about how they were fried, cooked, and dead they were when they got an A by a smaller margin they would've liked. I got sick of, and remain sick of, the faux panic that seems to grip my peers like a toddler who got ahold of drain cleaner[^3]. A separate, similarly sickening phenomena I've had to deal with is the way everyone here is so used to being right that they treat their own words like gospel[^4]. Let me be clear, I don't see myself as purer than my peers because I have less confidence in my answers than they do. In fact, I'm often swinging in the opposite direction where I don't lend myself enough credibility. That said, it's so unbelievably frustrating to discuss psets with someone who assumes they're right by default because they:
- seldom justify their answers when I press them for the intuitive/rigorous reasons for the approaches they took. Though intuition suffices for their purposes, it doesn't suffice for me because I don't like an answer I can't defend end-to-end[^5]
- assume I'm dumber than them, because if they thought I was their equal, they'd be more cautious before preempting my mistakes
- spend more time defending their answers and approaches than considering what assumptions they make about the problem and their approach, wasting both of our times

Suffice to say it's frustrating and has done horrid damage to my self confidence in my problem-solving abilities. Yeah yeah, run-your-own-race and allat but assuredly the only people shoving that mantra down my throat are going to be the ones not dealing with it day-in day-out. Obviously I'm trying to do right by me, but that doesn't take away from the erosion I feel during the academic year. 

Maybe it's taken me too long to realize, or maybe to put it into words, but I think the words I'd put to this phenomena are intellectual immodesty. It's a scourge and I despise it wholly. It's the bane of my existence at MIT. I understand fully this is a super first-world ivory-tower problem, but it doesn't change the fact that my self esteem is consistently on the floor whenever the semester kicks up. It's so so so demoralizing when the rare psets I do without external help, satisfied with my problem-solving, are pridefully decried as unstimulating by my classmates the day after they're due. It's not just that so many people insist of their own brilliance, it's that they feel this pathological compulsion to express it, as if declaring it lets them gasp for air. I don't understand it. Dude, I know you're a genius, I don't know why you still feel like you have to insist it. What's funny/sad too is the dogma cooonstantly expressed by the professors and admin that each student ought to act like they're running their own race, but they only really emphasize that to the dunces (e.g., me) doing poorly in their classes. It's a strange culture of low-key empowering the showboaters to showboat and only telling the comparative failures that their struggles are ultimately trivial. 

Kinda on that note, I'm inclined to recall the first meeting I had with [her](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/hUAwg7qp2DM/sddefault.jpg) where she talked to me like a little kid who isn't learning his times tables as quickly as the rest of the class[^6]. Dude, she looked at me like I was at risk for flipping the table when she told me after bombing my first two quizzes that I likely wouldn't be able to get an A. My sister in Christ, I'm aware of this fact, and I truly truly truly only want to pass and restrict the length of time I wake up in cold sweats induced by nightmares of your course to only 2 decades. I certainly sound dramatic, but right-hand-on-the-Bible, this is more or less how each my meetings went with her. I'm not sure what they were for. She likely credits them for the reason I didn't drop the class and that's probably true. Doesn't change how ridiculous the whole situation was. 

Mayhaps it's an odd pull, but I strongly suspect that the reason for the "don't kick the puppy" attitude she had was because she understood me as being similarly intellectually immodest as most of my peers are. Again, that's really the opposite of my problem. I really struggle to build momentum in my problem solving because of this mental block that won't let me progress if I can't justify every little $\mathrm ds$ I take when solving a problem. It's happened to me innumerably many times where I can see the next logical step of a problem, but I can't justify it intuitively or convince myself of the logical leap, so I don't take it in fear of wasting my time. That is, I mistrust my instincts and I suffer immensely for it.

I dread having to deal with that again, as much as I love my peers and the chance to solve hard problems. I think my ability to solve problems has tangibly improved, but I really have to trust my instincts and convince myself that pushing back is equal parts expected and necessary if I'm not going to survive. I'll be retaking structures, and I'm fortunate enough to be taking it with [the goat](https://tiukhtikova.space/) since she's doing Unified out of order, having taken the "second" half of it last semester and is now taking the "first" half this semester. I'm also slated to take satellite engineering with her, so I'm looking forward to that too. 

This feels like one of those posts where anyone going through it would be able to say it doens't require a lot of thought to reach the conclusions I have, but I think there's a clear distinction between understanding what people do and figuring out how to put it into words. I don't fancy myself a philosopher, but c'mon, "intellectual immodesty" is a pretty damn clever. It's a devil, to be clear, but clearly I need to make a few deals with it under the table if I'm going to make this next semester work. I'll also need to not be writing blog posts at 2 a.m., but I digress. I'm going to the beach and Trader Joe's tomorrow, so I'll close this off now and go to sleep. I've had this on my mind for a long time and I desparately needed to put this all into words before the next time it eats me alive.

### Drag is Being Difficult
Holy cannoli I'm learning so much about Orekit and I'm learning how to better use the tool. Yay! I've gotten to a point where I can comfortably read through the orekit docs and adapt the Java snippets into Python. This is particularly nice, but something I realize I'm dealing with, which the oh-so-wonderful forums don't address is the fact that drag models are sooooo computationally expensive. I tried a morbillion times to simulate an aerocapture maneuver and I've had no success because those simulations only seem to warm up my CPU and I've yet to get one to run to completion.

To put things into perspective, when propagating an orbit with only drag as an extra consideration, lowering my perigee by just 5 kilometers (i.e., 100 km to 95 km) raises the execution time of the simulation from a few seconds to a length of time at least 3 hours long. I know that some of it has to do with how sensitive the diff eqs become when dealing with an atmosphere that varies exponentially with height, but JESUS. I've done experimenting with the scripts to show that transitioning from a hyperbolic to Keplerian orbit is possible, and I've also experimented to show that aerobraking works just fine to lower an orbit, or even crash the satellite after enough passes through the top of the atmosphere. However, varying the parameters just slightly from these working examples into something more closely resembling an aerocapture maneuver completely bricks the simulation. 

My hope is that I can find some other propagator that specializes in this or write a custom integrator that handles these sensitive integrations a lot more gracefully than the ones Hipparchus has for me. I'll probably make my next two tutorials on aerobraking and transitioning from hyperbolic to Keplerian orbits. I think the latter will be especially interesting to explore because I've gotten to the point where I can take a hyperbolic orbit and lower its energy enough to become a Keplerian orbit with basically all the same properties, minus a positive semi-major axis and eccentricity less than 1, but I wonder if I can take an arbitrary hyperbolic orbit and transition into an arbitrary Keplerian orbit. I could include a segment for a bielliptic transfer being performed to save fuel when changing the inclination. Oooouuuu maybe I could do something like including extra plots to show the savings from that kind of maneuver...

I'm having a lot of fun with this project and I'm learning a lot. Maybe in the next post I'll punch in the plots I alluded to earlier. Astrodynamics is cool.
