The watch arrived yesterday and oh my LORD :star_struck: For far too long I've been struggling with my phone screen during my runs. It's not annoying to carry around, but it *is* annoying to try pausing/finishing a run or checking my route when my fingers are sweating my phone screen refuses to work with me. It's unavoidable that sweaty fingers will misbehave with a phone screen by nature of water on phone screens, but that doesn't change how annoying it is to deal with. 

I was on a video call with my [illustrious wife](/assets/images/liz.webp) when I was unboxing and setting up the watch. It was a beautifully simple process and within a short while I was able to go on a calm 7.5 mile run. Apparently I run all my mileage like a freak because Despite progressing at what I thougth was a reasonable pace, the watch told me I was living in zones 4 and 5 :skull: I think I generally have a high heart rate because it feels sus that a run which felt mostly calm would have taken such a high toll on me. That said, it's only one data point for the watch to draw conclusions from, so we'll see how things shake out from there.

I like the interface of the Coros Pace 4. It's a few physical, tactile buttons and it does exactly what I need it to. When I'm on runs I do intermittently peek at the stats of how fast I'm going, and it's so much easier to do with the watch. It's not something I'd say had me thinking "oh my gawsh this is life changing" every time I raised my wrist. In fact, I'd say the best part of the experience was how little I had to think about checking the information on my watch face. There was no "pray face ID works because your fingers are too sweaty to interact with your screen," it was just looking at my wrist or clicking a button as needed. 

There's a bajillion reviews of the Coros Pace 4 online, but the long and short of why I picked this one particularly is because it will:
- last me a long time thanks to Coros taking care to send firmware and feature updates regularly for years after a product releases
- do everything I need it to and nothing more. When I was comparing it with its equivalent Garmin products, I noticed that the Garmin watches kept track of so many different health metrics that I just didn't care about.
- require me to think very little about taking care of it. The battery lasts significantly longer than its equivalent Garmin counterparts, which means it'll be easier to take care of as I just have to charge it once a week when it's still well above 50% charge
- do me right with the GPS performing well in environments that are usually hostile to smart watches (e.g., between high rise buildings). Having a product that works wherever I use it is :heart_eyes:
- pay for itself quickly in the ease of use and longevity. I don't care about having a fancy ecosystem like Garmin watches, but everything I do care about this watch mathces or outperforms its Garmin equivalent at a cheaper price
- make me lik the goat Jakob Ingebrigsten, who uses it. 

These aren't terribly specific examples because I ain't finna do no comprehensive review with charts n shi. That's what the other online reviews are for.

Liz will be the first one to tell you I haven't been this excited for a new toy since I was a little kid waiting for the [Power Rangers Megazords](https://www.ebay.com/itm/147481757576?chn=ps&mkevt=1&mkcid=28&google_free_listing_action=view_item&srsltid=AfmBOorlDAL5U-hgB32LFZQHn2__e49R80I1ENMmI46PhhhKz9ez8X2kMts) my older brother ordered from eBay for me. I was checking downstairs every half hour after 2 pm the day it was scheduled to arrive xD. Truly I was acting similarly to when I'm waiting for Liz at the airport HAHA. I can't help it, it's rare that I splurge on myself like this. My mom and Liz will be the first ones to start popping champagne when I finally make the choice to replace the pair of shoes I've been wearing as they fall apart [^1]. Hell, earlier this summer when I bought 2 new pairs of shorts because I couldn't deal with the heat I shocked my family. 

Suffice to say, I'm a *fairly* frugal individual. I don't like buying stuff I won't use, and the stuff I do have is copmrised of either keepsakes or things I'll use until I die. Another example I just thought of are running shoes I use. I've had the same pair of Rebel v4s since high school, but idc how worn out and beat up they get because they keep doing what I need them to. Let it be known I do have another pair of new shoes that I take out for faster runs.This watch is definitely in the latter category. Hell, when this thing's battery starts degrading, I'm going to take it apart and replace it. I'm going to pull a ship of Theseus on this thing because I refuse to let it die.

I look forward to using this thing for my runs going forward. Having a fun little toy to pace mysel with or do workouts with will hopefully make finding motivation to continue working out easier when the schoolyear starts. I do have the half-marathon waiting for me in November, but hopefully that's just the beginning of project sub 4:50. I got to a 4:54 at altitude, which converts to 4:50 at sea level. I have a ways to go before I'm that fit again, but I'm older and with a higher physicality ceiling than I did when I graduated high school, so I think it's doable. Inshallah I will break that barrier.
